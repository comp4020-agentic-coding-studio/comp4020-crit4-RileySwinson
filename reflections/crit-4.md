# Crit 4 — An instrument

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Remembering the video. I watched CodeParade's Fractal Sound Explorer years ago,
and the thing that surfaced was not the visuals but the mechanism: you do not
map the fractal onto a synthesiser, you let the orbit *be* the waveform. Once I
had that back, almost every decision fell out of it. The pitch is not something
I chose and assigned — it is rate ÷ period, and the period belongs to the point,
not to me. That is why the instrument is expressive without me having designed
any expression into it: the set already contains every sound it can make, and
playing it is a matter of finding them.

It also meant I could tell whether the build was correct rather than merely
plausible. A period-3 orbit had to sound at a third of the step rate. It did.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It reopened something I have been putting off for years. I want to learn the
mathematics of fractals properly again — not enough to make a picture, enough to
know what I am looking at — because they are genuinely beautiful and I have been
treating that as a hobby I would get to later. And I want to be good at graphics
programming, good enough to make fractal visualisations that are both beautiful
and *controlled*, where the look is a decision rather than a happy accident.

I have wanted this for a long time and never found the time. Getting an
instrument out of the Mandelbrot set in an afternoon made the gap between
wanting it and doing it look much smaller than I had assumed.
